# Stage 12 — Marketplace Validation Framework

**Status:** Framework ready for founder use
**Prerequisite:** Stages 7–11 complete (verified core quotation, security, email delivery, database/CI/deploy)
**Goal:** Produce a written GO / NARROW / STOP decision backed by real buyer and provider evidence — before building any marketplace software.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document provides everything a founder needs to validate marketplace demand manually. No software is built in Stage 12. The output is recorded evidence from real interviews and a concierge pilot, measured against pre-agreed thresholds.

**Why concierge first:** Building marketplace software before proving that buyers will post real RFQs, providers will respond, and selections will happen is the most common failure mode. A manual pilot costs only time and tests the actual value proposition.

---

## 2. Interview Plan

### 2.1 Target counts

| Participant type | Minimum interviews | Target interviews |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers (businesses that need services) | 8 | 15+ |
| Providers (businesses that deliver services) | 8 | 15+ |

Interviews should be conducted in person or via phone/video, not by email survey. Each interview lasts 20–30 minutes.

### 2.2 Buyer Interview Questions

Record the interviewee's name, business name, location, and contact method.

**Current quote/procurement process:**
1. When you need a service (e.g., printing, branding, signage, fabrication), how do you currently find providers?
2. Do you call around, ask for referrals, post on social media, or something else?
3. How many providers do you typically contact for a single job?
4. How do you compare proposals — price, timeline, quality, relationship?
5. What does the process look like from "I need this" to "I've chosen a provider"? Walk me through the last time.

**Pain points:**
6. What is the most frustrating part of finding and comparing providers?
7. How long does it typically take from needing a service to getting quotes?
8. Have you ever chosen a provider without comparing options? Why?
9. Have you ever been surprised by a price after agreeing? What happened?
10. What information is usually missing from quotes you receive?

**Willingness to use a marketplace:**
11. If there were a platform where you posted what you need and received proposals from vetted providers, would you use it?
12. What would make you trust the providers on the platform?
13. Would you prefer to see all proposals yourself, or have someone curate the best 2–3?
14. What would stop you from using it?

**Budget ranges:**
15. What is the typical range for the jobs you need done? (e.g., under 10k, 10k–50k, 50k–200k, over 200k KES)
16. Do you pay upfront, on delivery, or in milestones?
17. Have you ever paid for a quote or to access providers? Would you?

**Response time expectations:**
18. How quickly do you need proposals after posting a request?
19. What is the longest you would wait before going back to your current method?
20. If you received proposals within 24 hours, would that change your behavior?

### 2.3 Provider Interview Questions

Record the interviewee's name, business name, service categories, coverage areas, and contact method.

**Current client acquisition:**
1. How do you currently find new clients? Walk me through your last 3 clients.
2. What percentage of your work comes from referrals vs. direct outreach vs. repeat clients?
3. How much time do you spend on finding new work per week?
4. What is your typical response when someone calls asking for a quote?

**Capacity:**
5. How many jobs can you take on per month at your current capacity?
6. What months are your busiest? What months are slowest?
7. Do you turn down work? How often, and why?
8. What is your typical job size range?

**Willingness to pay for leads:**
9. If someone sent you qualified RFQs (requests for quote) matched to your services and area, would you want to receive them?
10. Would you pay per lead, per month, or per successful match?
11. What would a qualified lead be worth to you? (fixed amount or percentage of job value)
12. What would make you stop trusting the platform?

**Service categories and coverage:**
13. What services do you offer? Be specific (e.g., "offset printing up to A1", "vinyl banner signage", "laser cutting up to 6mm steel").
14. What geographic areas do you serve? How far will you travel?
15. Do you have minimum order sizes or job thresholds?
16. Are there services adjacent to yours that clients often ask for together?

### 2.4 Interview recording

For each interview, record:
- Date and method (in-person, phone, video)
- Interviewee name and business
- Key quotes (verbatim, with permission)
- Answers to willingness questions (would use / would pay / response time)
- Any spontaneous comments about competitors or alternatives
- Follow-up consent (can we contact you for the concierge pilot?)

Store interview records in a secure folder. Do not publish individual responses. Aggregate findings in the decision summary.

## 3. Concierge Pilot Procedure

The concierge pilot is fully manual. The founder (or an operator) acts as the marketplace — matching buyers to providers, forwarding RFQs, collecting proposals, and presenting them. No software is built.

### Step 1: Collect RFQ from a buyer

**How:** The buyer contacts the founder via phone, WhatsApp, or a simple Google Form. The founder fills in a structured RFQ record (see Section 7).

**Required fields:**
- Buyer name and contact
- Service category needed
- Scope description (what exactly is needed)
- Location (where the work is done or delivered)
- Timing (when needed, flexibility)
- Budget range (optional but encouraged)
- Any attachments (photos, specs, existing samples)
- Deadline for proposals (suggested: 48–72 hours)

**Validation:** The founder confirms the RFQ is genuine by speaking to the buyer. No anonymous or vague requests.

### Step 2: Match to 2–3 providers manually

**How:** The founder reviews the provider list (from interviews or outreach) and selects providers whose:
- Service categories match the RFQ
- Coverage areas include the RFQ location
- Capacity is available (confirmed by a quick check)
- Verification status is at least "known" (referenced by at least one other trusted provider or buyer)

**Recording:** Log which providers were selected and why. Log providers who were considered but not selected, and why not.

### Step 3: Send RFQ to providers

**How:** The founder sends the RFQ details to each selected provider via email or WhatsApp. Each provider receives the same RFQ details but does NOT know who else was invited.

**Provider message includes:**
- RFQ scope, category, location, timing, budget range
- Deadline for proposal submission
- Instructions: "Reply with your proposed price, timeline, and any questions"
- Statement: "You are one of a small number of providers invited. Other invitees are not disclosed."

**Recording:** Log the date/time each provider was contacted and the method.

### Step 4: Collect proposals manually

**How:** Providers reply via email or WhatsApp. The founder records each proposal in the tracking sheet.

**Proposal fields captured:**
- Provider name
- Proposed price (or price range)
- Timeline (when they can do it, how long it takes)
- Any conditions, questions, or exclusions
- Confidence level (firm quote vs. estimate subject to site visit)

**Deadline enforcement:** If a provider has not responded by the deadline, the founder sends one reminder. If still no response, mark as "no response" and proceed with the proposals received.

### Step 5: Present proposals to buyer

**How:** The founder compiles the proposals into a simple comparison (text message, email, or a shared document) and sends it to the buyer.

**Presentation rules:**
- Each proposal is presented with provider name, price, timeline, and notes
- The buyer sees only the proposals received for their RFQ
- The founder does not recommend a specific provider unless the buyer asks
- The buyer is told how many providers were invited (but not which ones did not respond)

### Step 6: Record selection outcome

**How:** When the buyer selects a provider (or declines all), the founder records:
- Which provider was selected (or "none selected")
- Reason for selection (if shared by buyer)
- Whether the work proceeded
- Any issues encountered during delivery (follow up later)

### Step 7: Follow up after 30 days for repeat intent

**How:** 30 days after the RFQ was posted, the founder contacts the buyer:
- Did the work get completed?
- Are you satisfied with the outcome?
- Would you use this process again for your next need?
- Do you have another RFQ right now?

**Recording:** Log the repeat-intent answer as YES / MAYBE / NO.

## 4. Success / Stop Thresholds

These thresholds are templates. The founder must finalize the actual numbers before starting the pilot and commit to them in writing.

| Metric | Definition | Minimum for GO | Stop threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Completed RFQs | RFQs that go through the full concierge cycle (Step 1–6) | **X** in 8 weeks (suggested: 15) | Fewer than 8 in 8 weeks |
| Qualified response rate | Percentage of invited providers who submit a proposal by the deadline | **> Y%** (suggested: 70%) | Below 40% |
| Selection rate | Percentage of completed RFQs where the buyer selects a provider | **> Z%** (suggested: 60%) | Below 30% |
| Response time | Median time from RFQ sent to first proposal received | **< N hours** (suggested: 24) | Median > 72 hours |
| Repeat intent | Percentage of buyers who say they would use the process again (Step 7) | **> P%** (suggested: 50%) | Below 25% |
| Provider willingness to pay | Percentage of providers who say they would pay for lead access | **> Q%** (suggested: 40%) | Below 15% |

**Additional qualitative signals to record:**
- Are buyers asking for the service without being prompted? (pull vs. push)
- Are providers asking to be invited to more RFQs?
- Is the founder spending more time matching than the value justifies?
- Are there consistent categories and geographies where matches are easy?

### Threshold finalization

Before the pilot begins, the founder writes:

> "I commit to the following thresholds: [filled in]. I will record a GO, NARROW, or STOP decision after 8 weeks or after 20 completed RFQs, whichever comes first. I will not move the goalposts."

## 5. Decision Framework

After the pilot period (or after reaching the target RFQ count), the founder reviews the recorded evidence against the thresholds and records a written decision.

### GO — Build the narrow marketplace software pilot (Stage 13)

**All of the following must be true:**
- Completed RFQs meet or exceed the minimum threshold
- Qualified response rate meets or exceeds the GO threshold
- Selection rate meets or exceeds the GO threshold
- Response time meets or exceeds the GO threshold
- Repeat intent meets or exceeds the GO threshold
- Provider willingness to pay meets or exceeds the GO threshold
- At least one service category and one geography show consistent, repeatable matching
- The founder has identified a pilot cohort of at least 5 buyers and 5 providers willing to use a software version

**If GO:** Proceed to Stage 13 with the identified category, geography, and cohort. Document the approved scope explicitly.

### NARROW — Narrow the scope and repeat the concierge pilot

**Any of the following:**
- Overall thresholds are not fully met, but one specific category or geography shows strong signals (selection rate and repeat intent above threshold for that segment)
- Response time or response rate is weak overall but strong for a subset of providers
- Buyers are interested but the scope needs to be narrower (one category, one city) to reach liquidity

**If NARROW:**
1. Document the narrowed category and/or geography
2. Define new thresholds specific to the narrowed scope
3. Repeat the concierge pilot for 4–6 weeks focused only on that scope
4. Record a new GO or STOP decision after the narrowed pilot

### STOP — Do not build marketplace software

**Any of the following:**
- Completed RFQs fall below the stop threshold
- Selection rate falls below the stop threshold
- Repeat intent falls below the stop threshold
- Provider willingness to pay falls below the stop threshold
- The founder cannot identify any category or geography with viable matching
- The operational cost of concierge matching exceeds the perceived value

**If STOP:**
1. Document the evidence and the decision
2. Leave Stage 13 unstarted
3. Revise the product direction — focus on the core quote/SaaS product
4. Do not re-attempt marketplace validation without a significant change in market conditions or product positioning

## 6. Privacy Rules for Private Invitations

These rules apply to both the concierge pilot and any future software pilot.

### Provider privacy
- A provider invited to an RFQ **cannot see** which other providers were invited
- A provider **cannot see** other providers' proposals
- A provider **cannot see** whether the buyer selected another provider (they are told only "selected" or "not selected" for their own proposal)
- Provider contact details are **not shared with the buyer** until the buyer selects that provider's proposal (in the concierge pilot, the founder connects them)

### Buyer privacy
- The buyer sees **only the proposals submitted for their own RFQ**
- The buyer **cannot browse** providers or see provider profiles beyond what is in the proposal
- The buyer's contact details are **not shared with providers** in the proposal stage (the founder forwards proposals anonymously if the buyer prefers)

### Platform operator privacy
- The founder/operator can see all data during the concierge pilot (this is the definition of concierge)
- In the software pilot (Stage 13), platform admins can see marketplace data only through audited moderation views, not through casual browsing
- All admin access to marketplace data is logged

### Data handling
- RFQ details, proposals, and selection outcomes are stored securely
- No marketplace participant data is shared with third parties
- Participants can request deletion of their data (with quote/financial records retained per legal requirements)

## 8. Pre-pilot checklist

Before starting the concierge pilot, the founder confirms:

- [ ] Thresholds are finalized and committed in writing (Section 4)
- [ ] At least 8 providers have been interviewed and added to the provider list
- [ ] At least 8 buyers have been interviewed and expressed willingness to submit an RFQ
- [ ] The RFQ tracking sheet is set up (Section 7)
- [ ] The founder has decided on the initial service category and geography focus
- [ ] Privacy rules are understood (Section 6)
- [ ] The 8-week clock (or 20-RFQ target) has a defined start date
- [ ] The founder has blocked time for daily matching and proposal forwarding

---

## 9. Post-pilot decision record

After the pilot period, the founder writes a decision document containing:

1. **Pilot dates:** Start and end
2. **Thresholds committed:** The numbers from Section 4
3. **Actual results:** The summary metrics from Section 7
4. **Qualitative findings:** Key quotes, patterns, surprises
5. **Category/geography analysis:** Which segments worked, which didn't
6. **Decision:** GO / NARROW / STOP (one word, with the criteria from Section 5)
7. **Next steps:** What happens next, with a date
8. **Sign-off:** Founder name and date

This decision record is stored in `docs/marketplace/DECISION_RECORD.md` and referenced when Stage 13 begins (or is explicitly deferred).
